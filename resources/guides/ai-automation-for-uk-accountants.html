AI automation for UK accountants: where to start

Guide · Accountancy practices

Accountancy practices handle a steady stream of documents, client emails and deadlines — a good fit for automation, as long as client confidentiality and accuracy come first.

Where it helps

  • Document capture. Receipts, invoices and statements read and filed automatically, instead of keyed in by hand.
  • Chasing missing records. Automatic reminders to clients for the documents you're still waiting for. See Compliance Document Chasing.
  • Email triage. Client email sorted by type and urgency, so the important messages surface first.
  • First-draft summaries. Meeting and call notes turned into action lists for a person to check.

Where to be careful

  • Client confidentiality and UK GDPR. Know where documents are processed and by whom, and make sure appropriate contracts are in place with any provider that touches client data.
  • Accuracy. AI can misread or misclassify. Keep a human review for low-confidence items and anything that affects figures you'll file or report.
  • Professional standards. Check your professional body's current guidance on using AI in practice.

A sensible first step

Start with one contained workflow — for example, incoming receipts flowing into a review queue and then into your bookkeeping software. Measure the time it saves and the errors it catches, then extend from there.
