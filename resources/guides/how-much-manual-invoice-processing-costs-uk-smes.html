How much does manual invoice processing cost a UK small business?

Guide · Finance & Cash Flow

There's no single industry-average figure worth trusting here: the real cost depends on how many invoices you handle, who handles them, and how often something goes wrong. What you can do is work out your own number in about ten minutes.

The three costs to count

  1. Time. Minutes per invoice × invoices per month × the hourly cost of whoever does the work (their pay plus employer costs, or what your own time is worth).
  2. Errors and rework. Mistyped amounts, missed VAT, duplicate entries. Count how often something has to be corrected, and how long each correction takes.
  3. Cash flow. Invoices you send by hand tend to go out late, and chasing depends on someone remembering. Every day an invoice is paid late is a day you're financing your customer.

A worked example (made-up numbers)

These figures are invented to show the method. They are not an average, and yours will differ.

Suppose you process 60 supplier invoices and receipts a month. Each takes 6 minutes to open, read, key in and file, and the person doing it costs £25 an hour.

  • Handling time: 60 × 6 minutes = 360 minutes = 6 hours, which is £150 a month.
  • Corrections: 3 a month at 15 minutes each is 45 minutes, about £19 a month.
  • Direct time cost: roughly £170 a month, or about £2,000 a year.

That's before counting the sales side, such as chasing late-paying customers.

What that number doesn't capture

Books that lag behind mean slower decisions. Quarter-end crunches pull people off more valuable work. And the awkwardness of chasing a customer is a cost too, even if it never appears on a timesheet.

Where automation helps — and where it doesn't

AI invoice and receipt processing cuts the keying and filing. Invoice chasing automation takes reminders off your plate. Neither removes the need for a human check on unusual invoices, which is why a good setup queues uncertain items for review rather than filing them blindly.
